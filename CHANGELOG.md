# Changelog

## Unreleased

The dynasty update for CEO runs. Artists grow through up-and-comer, star and superstar tiers, with careers to level 30 and perks along the way; a superstar can lift or sink the company's value, and a loyal one follows you to a second company if you are fired. Pre-release listening parties let you rework an album before it goes out. Buildings and floors go to level 10 with renovations, and department heads and team members grow with time, training and promotions.

Thirty authored artists, ten department heads, six team members and six directors arrive through scouting and drives, each with a story; artists, heads, team members, directors and the label itself live through chains of decision cards. Every big change can explain itself, and the Wallet keeps a monthly ledger. A starter checklist and a fast first album open a new run.

Three rival labels release, fight chart battles, bid for your stars, feud with you and can be bought. Four music eras arrive at different times in each run and change what pays: albums, singles, touring, licensing or the catalog, with a warning and an early investment. Old albums keep earning as catalog and classics, and bring deluxe editions, reissues, samples, syncs and greatest hits through the Vault. A hall of fame and 33 achievements carry across runs and travel with exported saves, six challenge starts include a weekly shared seed, and small hall of fame perks unlock along the way. New Game+ points in CEO runs are now prestige points. From phase 3 renewals, tours, department chores and small company decisions can be delegated, never stories, superstars or the board.

A board now lets a minimum album budget through each period, and a good review is a chance to negotiate it up. A label with no artists can always sign one up-and-comer. On phones the top bar takes a seventh of the screen instead of a quarter. Saves of long CEO runs with high-level artists or a public company load again.

Rivals keep pace with the label: their records are measured against yours as well as the phase, beaten rivals recover and runaway ones settle, a strong rival can take over a weak one, and a rival that leaves the charts is replaced by a new label. The studio can make one album at a time at phase 1, two at phases 2–4 and three at phases 5–6, so artists waiting on it tour, and the head of Live books tours only while the studio is full. From phase 5 the label can buy into Tributary, a streaming platform that lifts its sales and chart numbers, though big stakes draw questions from the press.

## v2.0.0

The CEO update. New labels now start in CEO mode: you run the company from a broke home studio that can only find local artists to a global label that can go public. The company grows through six phases with goals for fame, revenue, releases and hits, and builds up in separate buildings along a side-scrolling street instead of one lot. A phone holds the Inbox, Calendar, Contacts, Wallet and Settings. The label can be named and renamed.

Departments (A&R, Marketing, Promotion, Live, Business Affairs, Finance, Creative) each need a head, and heads with ambition and influence may try a power grab: gossip first, then a co-CEO or, at worst, your chair. Each artist has a team (manager, producer, A&R rep, publicist, stylist, tour manager, business manager) whose chemistry changes records, fame and tours, plus traits and a main want that drive morale and walk-outs.

A bank loan brings no board. An angel or venture round brings directors with their own agendas, a yearly review that sets your budget, perks and creative freedom, and the chance of a no-confidence vote. Being fired ends the run. Directors can be lobbied with meetings, promises and favors. Opportunities, crises and office politics arrive in the Inbox with deadlines. Revenue streams open by phase; the company has a value and shares, can list through an IPO with analyst targets, and can buy catalogs and smaller labels. CEO runs end with their own grade and New Game+ bonuses.

Classic mode is still offered and plays as 1.1. Classic saves can keep their rules or convert to CEO mode, with a backup of the original. The game can be installed as a web app and plays offline.

## v1.1.0

The menu bar is gone. Everything opens from a person or place on the lot. Tap an artist for their stats, contract, gigs, hot streak, albums, a new album and tour booking. Tap a staff member for their role and replacements. The lobby has a front desk for scouting and hiring and a trophy case for the awards; a new office holds campaign goals, finances and the discography; each recording room starts an album there; a + slot builds the next room; the roof sign shows rank, headquarters, pairings and the combo notebook. On the street, the billboard shows the charts and promotion, the newsstand the industry wire, and the tour bus the tours. Every recording location stands along the street, with its level and rent, and can start an album there. A roster in the side panel lists everyone, including artists on tour. The goal and "on fire" chips in the top bar open the office and the billboard.

Location boost text now includes the bonus better locations start with. Saves from 1.0 load unchanged.

## v1.0.0

The headquarters lot is now the main screen, with a live clock (pause, 1×, 2×, 4×) that stops for events, album decisions, milestones and open menus. A run lasts 20 years with a goal each year, a yearly review, a final score graded one to five stars, and New Game+ with legacy bonuses bought from that score. Albums can be recorded at ten locations that level up, reveal signature themes and suit some genres more than others. Idle artists can play paid gigs. A new Promotion screen adds hot streaks, lead singles and ad campaigns.

Dropping below $0 now starts a four-week grace period with a warning card instead of ending the run at once, and staff can be let go while in the red. Shop and bar takings stop growing past 400 label fame, and the Master Archive pays on the best 30 finished albums. Better locations start with stronger stage boosts. Score rows are capped and the star bars raised, so five stars needs a strong run.

Saves from 0.9 keep playing: a label that finished year one continues into year two, and earlier years' goals are read from history where it can tell. Long saves trim old chart and award detail at each year end so they stay within browser storage. A save that fails the load checks is kept as a backup before a new label replaces it.
