# Game rules

Every new label is a CEO run. Saves from before 2.0 can keep their original rules or convert, with a backup of the original.

## Getting around

There are no menus. Everything opens from a person or place on the lot, in a panel beside it; time stops while the panel is open. The roster in the side panel lists every artist and staff member, including artists away on tour.

Use the section buttons at the top of a busy panel to switch views. Find recording locations under **Out of town → Studios**, and start an album from an artist’s **Record** section or a studio. Production shows one decision at a time. Open **Rating breakdown** to see what goes into each estimate.

## Label ranks

Six ranks: Bedroom Label, Indie Imprint, Rising Label, Major Contender, Powerhouse and Greatest of All Time. Each rank lists goals for label fame, albums released, facilities built, best chart peak, awards won and cash on hand. Promotion happens at the weekly settlement once every goal is met. A rank is never lost.

| 2 Indie Imprint        |      3 | 4×2 |                1 | 30 fame, 2 albums, 1 facility                                  |
| 3 Rising Label         |      4 | 4×3 |                2 | 90 fame, 5 albums, 3 facilities, Top 40 peak, $20,000          |

Each rank-up from Indie Imprint onward grants two new album themes (three at Greatest of All Time). Ranks 2, 3 and 4 also unlock the room tiers that used to come from fame alone.

## Album themes and combos

Every new album takes one theme, or up to two from Rising Label. Six themes are known at the start; 24 exist. New themes come from development sessions (20% chance), completed tours, the Writers' Room and rank-ups.

Each theme fits each genre as Poor, Fair, Good or Great. The fit is hidden until you record that genre and theme together; after that it appears in the studio's combo notebook. Fit adds −5 / 0 / +4 / +7 to the Identity and Songwriting stages and to Fan response at launch. Two themes combine at 75% of their summed fit.

Releasing a combo levels it up, to a maximum of level 5. Each level above 1 adds one point to every stage. Repeating a combo within 26 weeks of an earlier release costs 4 Reach per recent release, up to 8. Albums without a theme still record but get no theme bonus.

The label is called Goat Records until you rename it in the office. A rename costs 15 fame while fans catch up; the new name shows on the roof sign, the charts, award results and the news.

No two people at the label share a name: artists, prospects, staff, executives, team members and directors each have their own.

- A theme reward pays $5,000 per theme instead once every theme is known.
- Cash below $0 at a weekly settlement starts a four-week grace period. A warning card pauses the clock; get back above $0 before the fifth settlement or the label folds and the run ends. A run that reaches the end of year 20 while in the red still completes.
- Goals are checked at each year boundary, right after the annual awards. A met goal pays its reward once: cash, label fame, or new album themes. Each year is scored only once.
- Goal types: reach a label rank, an album that peaked at a chart position (or several), awards won, albums released, hit albums, facilities built, label fame, cash on hand. "Total" goals count the whole run; "this year" goals count only that operating year's releases or awards.
- Each year end queues a yearly review: the goal result and reward, the year's numbers (releases, hits, awards, best chart position, fame gained, cash change, rank) and the next goal. Continue dismisses it.
- The current goal shows as a status chip and in the goal line. The office lists all 20 years with their goals, rewards and results, the score so far, legacy bonuses in use and earlier runs.
- The run ends once the year-20 awards ceremony is resolved. The final review shows the score breakdown, the star grade and highlights: best album, #1 albums, awards, top rank, peak cash, artists developed (career level 5+) and goals met.
- New Game+ opens when a run reaches the end of year 20, or folds after year one. The player picks legacy bonuses with legacy points; the new run starts at week 1 with a fresh roster and catalog, the run number raised and earlier runs listed.

Final score = rank × 100 + awards × 60 (up to 15 awards) + fame × 0.25 (up to 4,000 fame) + #1 albums × 80 (up to 10) + other Top 10 albums × 25 (up to 20) + hit albums × 10 (up to 40) + yearly goals met × 30 + 1 per $1,000 on hand (max 500). Each row stops counting at its cap, so no single source decides the grade. A run that folds loses 25% of that total.

Stars: 1 below 1,200, 2 from 1,200, 3 from 2,400, 4 from 3,600, 5 from 4,800.

Legacy points = floor(final score / 250) + points left unspent from the last New Game+. A strong run earns about 20, so buying every bonus takes two or three runs.

## Recording locations and paid gigs

### Locations

- An album may pick one location, or none. A location needs its label rank and hosts one album in production at a time.
- Rent is paid with the album budget when recording starts. It counts toward the album's total investment.
- A location adds its stage boosts to the stages it lists and its genre affinity to every stage. Bonuses lock at the level the location had when recording started.
- Each release there adds 1 experience, 2 for a hit (quality 75+). Levels raise the stage boosts and the rent.
- The first album recorded at a location reveals its signature theme and adds it to the label's themes if it is new. Later albums never add it again. The theme cannot be used on the album that found it.
- Each location records albums started, releases, experience, level and best quality.

### Location and gig formulas

- Affinity points per stage: Great +4, Good +2, Fair 0, Poor −3.
- Stage boost = listed value + level bonus + (location rank − 1). Level bonus by level 1–9: 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5. The rank term lets a new, better location start ahead of a fully leveled early one.
- Experience for levels 2–9: 2, 4, 7, 10, 14, 19, 25, 32.
- Rent = base rent × (1 + 0.05 × (level − 1)), rounded to $50.
- Gig pay = (base + min(fans, venue cap) × per fan + artist fame × per fame + craft × per craft) × (1 − spread + 2 × spread × roll), rounded to $10.

## Promotion: hot streaks, singles and ads

- **Hot streaks.** An artist catches fire after a hit release (quality 75+), a Top 10 chart week or an award win. Each album can trigger Top 10 heat once; each award counts once.
- A trigger adds its levels (max 3) and resets the timer to at least its weeks (max 12). A shorter trigger never cuts the timer. The timer counts down at every weekly settlement, except in the week the heat was added. At zero the streak ends.
- While on fire, every production stage the artist records shows an "On fire · N weeks left" row, and all their albums still in an earning run sell more.
- **Lead single.** Once Recording is done (three of five stages), an album in production can release one lead single. Not before Recording, not twice, not after the album is out. It costs by budget tier and raises the artist's fans and attention at once. At launch it adds a row to Fan response and Market impact (Critics ignore it) and boosts sales for the first three earning weeks.
- A single's reception (Quiet −2, Steady 0, Strong +2, Breakout +4) is drawn when it is released and added to both launch rows.
- **Ads.** A released album still in its earning run can run one ad at a time. Types unlock by label rank. The cost is paid at once, any fan, attention or reputation effect applies at once, and the sales boost starts with the next weekly settlement. An ad is clipped to the earning weeks left.
- All promotion actions are refused during an industry event or after the season ends.

### Promotion formulas

- Heat triggers: hit +1 level for 6 weeks; Top 10 week +1 level for 4 weeks; award +2 levels for 8 weeks.
- Stage bonus = heat level (up to +3).
- Heat sales = 1 + 0.06 × heat level (up to ×1.18).
- Single sales = ×1.15 while the album's earning age is below 3.
- Ad cost = base × (1 + label fame ÷ 1,000), rounded to $50.
- Promotion multiplier = min(1.6, heat × single × ad). It multiplies with the engine's own sales factors and campaign event boosts.

## Make an album

1. Scout and sign an artist on a 1-, 3- or 5-album contract. Artist age and listener audience are independent.
2. Start an album from the artist, a recording room or a recording location: choose the artist, ranked room, genre, album theme, audience, base budget, optional production/push add-ons and working title.
3. Complete five interactive stages: Creative Direction, Songwriting, Recording, Mix & Master, and Campaign Planning. Each has three approaches, numeric forecasts and staff bonuses.
4. Review each animated calculation, then accept and advance a week. Youth artists take an extra week at Recording. Each selected add-on adds one further week after Campaign Planning. Animations can be skipped and respect reduced-motion settings.
5. At final review, inspect Identity, Songwriting, Performance, Sound and Reach. Their rounded average determines quality, hits and award eligibility.
6. Rename the album and optionally reshuffle a selected area. A reshuffle rerolls only its random variation (-12 to +12); other contributions remain fixed. It can make the score worse.
7. Choose a release-day strategy: Press premiere favors Critics, Fan listening party favors Fan response, and Wide digital launch favors Market impact. The target gains 6, an additional skill-based bonus with its specialist hired, and other ratings lose 3.
8. Confirm release and watch Critics, Fan response and Market impact calculate. Reception sets a persistent revenue multiplier. Album income starts when you advance time. Production and release ratings remain in the catalog.

## Artist levels and reshuffles

New releases earn 3 career XP and development sessions earn 1. Ten levels use cumulative XP thresholds 0, 4, 10, 19, 31, 46, 64, 85, 109 and 136. Level 1 grants one reshuffle, level 2 grants two, and level 3+ grants three. The allowance locks when production begins. Saved XP is preserved; old level numbers are recalculated against the longer career curve.

## Run the label

Salaries and booth upkeep are paid weekly. Albums generate diminishing gross revenue over 12 earning weeks. Contract royalties and distribution fees are deducted before label income. Interactive events can extend campaigns to 18 earning weeks. Label rank sets roster limits: 2 at Bedroom Label, rising to 8 at Label Empire. Quality 75+ is a hit and increases booth prestige. Annual awards accept one released album per genre from the current operating year. Submit or replace entries before week 52 ends. The ceremony occurs when the year closes; quarterly industry spotlights remain separate.

Industry events occur every four weeks; rival encounters every eight. Resolve events before managing production. Delayed consequences appear in Industry wire. A run lasts 20 years with a goal each year; year one's goal is to reach Indie Imprint. Four weeks below $0 ends the run. See Campaign and New Game+.

## Paid scouting and recruitment

Artist search budgets: Local open call $500 (3 prospects, craft 38–63), Regional showcase $2,000 (4 prospects, craft 48–76), National talent search $6,000 (5 prospects, craft 60–86). Higher tiers also raise potential and existing fan reach. These are search fees; artist advances are separate. Lists remain available until replaced by another search. Initial prospects are free.

Each Team role has three candidates with individual skill, hiring fee and salary. Run a role-specific Local $400 / Industry $1,200 / Executive $3,000 drive to replace that candidate list. Skills range 35–60 / 50–78 / 68–92. A chosen candidate's skill controls stage expertise and business benefits. Replacements and dismissals cost two weeks' severance. Existing employees retain their roles and salary when an older save is upgraded.

## Artist contracts and value

Choose 1, 3 or 5 album commitments. Short deals have lower advances and higher base royalties (18%); 3-album deals use 15%, and 5-album deals use 12%. Career recognition can increase newly signed rates. Longer deals cost more up front. Each release consumes exactly one commitment. An expired artist keeps the roster slot, draws no roster salary and cannot record or train until renewed. Releasing a completed contract is penalty-free; ending an unfinished one reduces label reputation by 3.

Market value rises with craft, fans, artist fame, reputation, level, potential, career recognition and momentum. The office reports weekly cash flow and artist development ceilings. Training costs rise with repeated sessions; momentum decays, so attention requires upkeep. New albums pay 4–8% baseline distribution fees, reduced by VP expertise. Releases by the same artist less than eight weeks apart receive a demand penalty. Previously recorded albums retain their original revenue model. Renewal fees and salary are recalculated from current value/stats. Albums retain the royalty rate from their original recording contract even if the artist renews or leaves later.

## Weekly charts and album events

The charts publish the Top 300 from 320 rival albums plus eligible label releases. Twenty rival releases enter every four weeks. Six rival companies have company-filtered listings in Industry wire. Release day shows a provisional debut estimate; the next weekly settlement determines official rank, sales and rewards. Weak releases may remain outside the chart. Animated milestones are Top 300 / 100 / 40 / 20 / 10 / 5 / #1, each rewarded once per album. Chart units are fictional balance units based on gross sales divided by eight.

Eight rotating CEO scenarios cover distribution, festivals, licensing, talent exchanges, contract pressure, compilations, policy disputes and chart rivalries. Choices can change relationships, fees, recruiting, artist loyalty and temporary sales. Creative ownership promises set a lasting royalty floor for future renewals.

Annual awards allow one entry per genre, released in that operating year (weeks 1–52, 53–104, and so on). Replace a submission before the year closes. Your entry competes against rival nominees using quality, critics, fan response, chart achievement and jury variation. Results and scores persist. Ceremony attendance is a separate choice and cannot buy the judging outcome.

Each album's original run has interactive quarter checkpoints after earning weeks 3, 6, 9 and 12. Calendar-quarter spotlight events also occur at weeks 13, 26, 39 and 52 when a catalog album is active. Choose sales boosts, sponsorships, interviews, recovery time, deluxe editions and encores. Choices can change artist fame/reputation/trust, label reputation, revenue, and campaign length. Colliding events queue. Temporary boosts start the next week, stack to a maximum ×1.8, and extensions cap at 18 earning weeks. Catalog cards show active boosts and remaining weeks.

## Development pacing

Training is limited to once per artist every three weeks, requires an active contract and idle artist, consumes energy, and gets more expensive with repeated sessions. Base gains are 3 below craft 65, 2 below 80, and 1 above that; an Artist Development specialist with skill 65+ adds one point. The growth ceiling is limited by potential, artist level and label rank tier. A Talent Academy raises it further. Existing high craft is preserved, not reduced. New releases grant 3 XP; training grants 1. This keeps album experience meaningful and prevents repeated same-week development from reaching 100.

Thirty local-discovery and thirty national-headliner sample seasons averaged roughly craft 80 and 91 at year end; none reached 100. These repeatable policy samples measure pacing, not player enjoyment or final difficulty.

## Older saves

Cash, artist craft and catalog totals are preserved. Legacy artists receive three future album commitments; previous releases are not retroactively charged against the new contracts. Historical catalog royalties are not charged retroactively. Future earning weeks use the new royalty and sales rules. Production, contracts, staff pools, charts, cooldowns and event queues are all saved.

Saves from 0.8 and earlier open at the rank their fame had already earned (30 / 90 / 200 fame for ranks 2 / 3 / 4), with the six starter themes and an empty headquarters lot. Albums already in production or released keep their original calculations and have no theme.

## Expectations and morale

The album target is frozen at recording start. It is the rounded maximum of $4,000, 75% of artist market value, 95% of that artist's latest album label revenue to date, and 65% of this project's total investment. This target measures label album revenue after royalties and distribution, excluding tour income or award prizes.

Assessment occurs on the advance after the earning window closes, allowing week-12 encore choices to extend the run first. Meeting the target grants morale and reputation. Missing it reduces morale, artist reputation/fame and label reputation/fame in proportion to the shortfall. Assessment happens once; low morale also weakens subsequent production ratings. Idle artists recover morale slowly. Catalog cards show target, progress and final penalties. Old released albums are exempt from retroactive expectations.

## Tours: the tour bus

A recent album (within 26 weeks of release) qualifies with a Top 10 peak, $10,000 gross sales, or at least two earning weeks and 250 units in the last earning week. Each album can launch one tour. The headliner and any labelmate must be idle. Album contracts count albums; touring does not consume an album commitment.

| Territory | Show weeks | Solo setup | Solo weekly tour operations |
| --------- | ---------: | ---------: | --------------------------: |
| Regional  |          4 |     $4,500 |                        $800 |
| National  |          8 |    $14,000 |                      $2,200 |
| World     |         12 |    $35,000 |                      $5,000 |

Booking requires setup cash plus one week of tour operations. Setup is charged immediately, so tour net starts negative. Ticket revenue ramps as show weeks pass, with demand based on artist popularity and album quality. Forecasts are estimates: weekly variation, fatigue, events and early endings can change the result.

- Solo: highest headliner XP/fame/fan gains and some craft growth, energy drain 8 per show week; minimum booking energy 65.
- Labelmate: ticket potential x1.65 (plus partner demand), setup x1.35, operations x1.20, both artists unavailable for new albums or training, energy drain 2 each; minimum energy 45 each.
- Rival: ticket potential x1.25, setup x1.12, operations x1.10, energy drain 5; minimum energy 55. Events can unlock the rival as a normal signing candidate or risk the headliner leaving for the rival label.

Tour choices occur at scheduled reviews, capped at three per label week across all active tours. Recovery and promotions can run for several days before another review. Final days close automatically if the decision budget is used. Seven resolved days advance one label week, with album earnings and salaries settled once. Choices can add or cut dates, change tour quality sharply, insert recovery days, alter ticket demand or risk immediate cancellation. Rival partnership offers occur every seventh day. Daily operations and a 15% venue commission appear in tour costs. Tour checkpoints offer investment, recovery time or risky continuation. Recovery adds calendar time and operating costs without ticket revenue. Exhaustion, booking disruptions, risky decisions or rival poaching can end a tour early. Setup costs are not refunded; future operating costs and tickets stop. A manual End remaining tour dates option is also available.

The ledger animates cumulative net and cost recovery every day. It includes setup, tour operations and tour-event fees. Existing label salaries, album sales and awards remain outside this tour net. Break-even triggers a one-time celebration. Both artists are released from the recording lock when a labelmate tour ends.

Contracts, albums and tours carry on from year to year. A label that stays below $0 past the grace period folds.

## Finding active work

The side panel lists the roster and active work, linking to pending events, album stages and the tour bus. Album cards distinguish action-ready steps from production waiting time. The tour bus puts active tour ledgers before new bookings, with completed and ended tours collapsed below. Released albums use compact expandable rows, with earning campaigns sorted before completed runs. All costs, ratings and tour history remain available by expanding a row.

## Inspiration and album pacing

Every artist has 0–100 inspiration. New and migrated artists start at 60. Each confirmed album release resets that artist to 0. Inspiration recovers by 10 per label week while not recording, or 5 while touring. Recording pauses recovery; seven tour days still count as one label week. Development sessions do not refill inspiration.

| Inspiration | Recording readiness                               | Stage contribution |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -----------------: |
| 0–24        | Cannot start an album                             |                  — |
| 25–49       | Minimum inspiration; rushing risks weaker results |                −10 |
| 50–74       | Better odds of a decent album                     |                  0 |
| 75–89       | Better odds of a great album                      |                 +6 |
| 90–100      | Best odds of a classic album                      |                +12 |

Inspiration locks when recording begins and contributes visibly to all five stage calculations, including reshuffles. It changes the outcome distribution, not a guaranteed quality result: craft, expertise, room, choices, morale and variation still matter. Reference quality bands are decent 60+, great 80+, classic 90+. The existing hit threshold remains 75. From a release reset, idle artists need 3 weeks to start, 5 for the decent tier, 8 for great and 9 for classic. Tours double those recovery times.

The studio shows the selected artist’s meter, tier and recording bonus. Existing projects keep their original calculations without a retroactive inspiration modifier. Imported saves missing inspiration receive the initial 60 once; a saved zero stays zero.

## Tour timing and profitability (v0.7)

Each tour day settles its show or recovery costs and receipts. A tour pauses for a decision only when the scheduled review is due and the weekly budget allows it. The prompt identifies the day already settled. Your choice affects upcoming days; seven resolved calendar days still advance the label one week. A show-day ticket boost lasts exactly the specified upcoming show numbers, pauses during recovery, and is clipped to shows remaining. Boosts stack to at most ×2.5.

Scheduling recovery extends the calendar by the actual number of recovery days allowed before the 2× original-length cap. Recovery starts on the next advance, earns no tickets, charges normal daily operations and restores 9 energy per participating label artist per scheduled day. Recovery-day prompts cannot book recursive recovery. When a final-day prompt fits the weekly budget, it completes the tour without selling future boosts; otherwise the final date closes automatically. Itinerary changes display before/after duration; health interventions depend on actual energy. Twelve normal show situations use a shuffled saved bag, with three recovery scenarios, two closing scenarios, a fatigue intervention and the rival recruitment encounter.

Booking forecasts and live shows now share one daily ticket calculation. The quote includes setup, daily operations, 15% venue commission and an estimated break-even day, with no assumed paid boosts or extensions. Event fees, fatigue, date changes and future quality changes remain uncertain. Active ledgers project the finish and break-even using current quality, confirmed boosts and remaining show/recovery days. Regular label salaries and album income are separate from tour net. Touring demand and lineup still matter: a low-demand tour can quote a loss.

Upcoming ticket rates have been rebalanced for both new and ongoing tours. Already-settled receipts, spending, cash and elapsed dates stay intact. The browser retains a pre-0.7 backup before refreshing old pending prompts; unresolved rival recruitment/poaching offers are preserved. New event choices keep their timing data across reloads.

## Multi-day tour decisions (v0.8)

There are at most three tour decisions per label week **across all active tours combined**, including optional closing reviews. Daily ticket income, costs, energy and show-counted boosts continue to settle normally. Album and general industry events are separate from the tour-decision budget.

Most ordinary choices schedule a review three days later. Two- or three-show promotions defer the review until their show sequence has completed, with recovery days excluded from the show count. Recovery plans run through all scheduled recovery days and the next show before another review. Added legs usually schedule a review four days later; cuts and risky continuation usually schedule it two days later. Prompts state the target day and warn that the weekly cap can defer it. A final date may close sooner than the next planned review.

**Advance to next activity** skips quiet tour days and stops at a scheduled review, a weekly settlement, the end of the last tour or a required album decision. It never silently chooses an option. The active ledger identifies the next review target, any show-number hold and remaining weekly tour-decision budget. Concurrent tours receive reviews in turn; each tour keeps daily receipts while another tour awaits its review.

The current pending decision remains available during upgrade and counts toward the current week’s budget. Save data retains the review schedule and used budget across reloads; a pre-0.8 local backup is retained on first upgrade. Tour accounting and quoted gate rates remain unchanged from v0.7.

## CEO runs

The company starts with $2,000, a home studio, and full ownership. The Company panel lists phase goals. Phases open new scouting territory, buildings and floors, departments, and weekly income. The phase also sets roster capacity, theme slots and the recording and promotion unlocks (see One ladder below).

| Phase            | Unlocks                                               | New weekly income                   |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| 1 Home studio    | Apartment, local scouting, A&R                        | Beats and local downloads           |
| 2 Indie label    | Storefront, city scouting, angel funding              | Streams and merchandise             |
| 3 Regional label | Studio and office, regional scouting, venture funding | Distribution and radio              |
| 4 National label | Headquarters and venue, national scouting             | Sync and brand partnerships         |
| 5 Global label   | Media center and warehouse, worldwide scouting        | Publishing and catalog royalties    |
| 6 Public company | IPO, overseas offices, acquisitions                   | Catalog purchases can add royalties |

For phases 2–5, the goals are fame, revenue over the last 52 weeks, releases, and hits. Where an outside board exists, it must approve the phase change. Phases never go backward. The office shows weekly income by source and rent, insurance, and interest in the expense breakdown.

**Phone and events.** The Inbox shows current and queued decisions. Once a quarter an eligible opportunity, crisis, or political problem can arrive. The label's phase, finances, roster, albums, staff, and board affect which offers can appear. A decision pauses the clock. Choose a response or defer to a later week. The choice card names the fallback that applies at expiry.

**Departments.** A&R (phase 1), Marketing and Promotion (phase 2), Live, Business Affairs and Finance (phase 3), Creative (phase 4). A recruitment drive for a head costs $600 × phase and finds three candidates, with skill set by the company's reach (local 30–55 up to world 65–92). A head earns $150 + $9 per skill point a week, costs two weeks' salary to sign and two weeks' severance to let go. Each head can add junior staff ($600 to hire, $300 a week), up to 2 × phase per department. Capacity is the head plus their staff.

Heads carry the old staff effects. A&R covers A&R and Producer, Marketing covers Marketing, Promotion covers Public Relations, Finance covers the VP and Creative covers Artist Development. A role uses the better of a hired employee and the head.

**Artist teams.** Manager and producer (phase 1), A&R rep (phase 2), publicist, stylist and tour manager (phase 3), business manager (phase 4). A drive costs $250 × phase. Members earn $80 + $5 per skill point a week and cost two weeks' pay to sign. Each artist has one member per role. A member can serve two artists at 60% effect each.

A member's effect is skill × role weight × chemistry. Chemistry runs from 0.6 to 1.4: +0.2 when they know the artist's genre (−0.15 when not), a fixed personality fit of up to ±0.2, and +0.05 per album released together (up to +0.2).

- Producer: +0.1 stage points per effect point on Recording and Mix & master.
- A&R rep: the same on Songwriting.
- Publicist: +0.05 artist fame per effect point on release; absorbs 0.4% of an event's trust loss per effect point, up to half.
- Tour manager: +0.2% ticket revenue per effect point; cuts 0.4% of tour energy drain per effect point, up to half.

**Wants and morale.** Each artist has fixed traits (ego, discipline, loyalty, work rate, 15–90) and one main want:

- Money: met for 26 weeks after a bonus (8 weeks of wage, at least $500).
- Creative control: met when their latest album's creative direction was Artist-led vision.
- Fame: checked every 13 weeks; met if fame rose by 3, or is 80 or more.
- Family time: met unless they have worked 8 weeks in a row; 2 idle weeks reset the count.

A met want adds 1 morale a week. An ignored one costs 3 + ego × 0.02 − loyalty × 0.015 (at least 1). Below 40 morale an artist is unhappy. With no albums left on their contract, an artist below 35 morale walks out at the next settlement and will not re-sign; loyalty of 75 or more lowers that line to 20.

**Funding and the board.** A CEO run starts owning all of itself, with no board.

- Bank loan, any phase, one at a time: $5,000 in phase 1 up to $1,200,000 in phase 6, repaid weekly over two years at 12% a year. It can be repaid early. A loan brings no shares and no board.
- Angel, phase 2 only and only once: 10% or 20% of the company for one board seat.
- Venture rounds (Series A, B, C), one in each of phases 3, 4 and 5: 15% or 30% for up to two seats, never more than the phase's board size (3, 5, 7).
- Rounds are priced at the larger of a phase floor ($60,000 in phase 2 up to $6,000,000 in phase 6) and a year of revenue × 1.5–4 by phase. Each round shrinks your share of what is left.

The board appears with the first angel or venture money. Each director is the Accountant, the Visionary, the Artist's Friend, the Old Guard or the Investor, with their own agenda and patience.

**Board confidence.** Each director's satisfaction is 50 plus their agenda's results, plus your relationship and any promises, kept between 0 and 100. Results are measured against targets set when the board forms (and at each review): weekly profit over 13 weeks, a year of revenue, cash, average artist morale, reputation, scandals since the review, and company value. Each metric is worth up to 20–25 points either way. Confidence is the average satisfaction across the seats. Each director's panel lists what moved them.

**The review.** With a board, a review comes every 52 weeks (26 once the label is public). It sets the next period:

- Budget: the first is the larger of a phase floor ($30,000 in phase 2 up to $4,000,000 in phase 6) and 60% of a year's revenue. After that, last budget × (0.8 + 0.4 × confidence ÷ 100) × (1 + revenue growth), with growth kept between −50% and +100%.
- Creative freedom: low below 40 confidence, medium below 70, high from 70. One step lower on probation.
- Perks: a company car from 55 ($90 a week), a penthouse office from 70 ($175), a private jet from 85 ($400).

Confidence below 35 at a review means probation for the next period. A no-confidence vote follows below 10 at any review, or below 20 while on probation. Each director under 25 satisfaction votes against you; the vote passes with more than half the seats, unless you own more than half the company. A lost vote is game over: you are fired. With a board, moving up a phase also needs 50 confidence.

**Budget and freedom in play.** Every payment counts against the period's budget; weekly costs do not. Past the budget, every director loses a point for each 2% of overage, up to 30. With medium or low creative freedom, a payment that would go past the budget needs the board's approval first. With low freedom, so do a signing whose fee is a quarter of the budget or more, an album with a risky theme (Rebellion, Revenge, Money, Party), and a genre the label has never released. A request goes to the Inbox; the board says yes at 50 confidence or more, and the approval lasts for the rest of that week.

**Lobbying.** Before a review you can work the board. Meeting a director takes your week (one meeting a week) and adds 4 to your relationship, up to 25. A promise picks a target from their agenda: today's figure plus 5 money steps, 10%, or 5 points (or no more scandals). It adds 2 at once; at the next review a kept promise adds 6 and a broken one costs 12. Favors add relationship too: hiring a director's pick as a department head (+8, any director; their pick has the lowest skill your reach finds), shelving an album in production with a risky theme (+10, the Old Guard), or giving up a perk until the next review (+6, the Accountant).

**The boardroom.** The office's boardroom card opens the board panel: confidence, budget, freedom and perks, each director with what moved them, lobbying, and funding. A review plays as a scene with the clock stopped: each director's score in turn, then the board's settings for the next period. With reduced motion the whole result shows at once.

**Power grabs.** Every 13 weeks each department head gains 2 influence, plus 1 for each director they have courted, up to 100. The chance a head starts a grab that quarter is max(0, (ambition + influence − loyalty − 90) ÷ 200) × (1 − your influence ÷ 200). Your influence is 20, plus 5 a phase, plus 10 for each perk. A grab first shows as office gossip in the Inbox and comes to a head 26 weeks later; meanwhile the executive courts one more director a quarter. Your answers:

- Promote: a "Senior" title, a 25% raise, 15 less ambition, and no grab for a year.
- Placate: a bonus of 8 weeks' salary; no new grab for a quarter, and a plot slips by a quarter.
- Sideline: move them to head an open department; influence −20, loyalty −15.
- Fire: the usual severance; every director they courted loses 6 relationship with you.
- Counter: win their board contacts back for $2,000 × phase and your week with the board; they lose 5 influence per contact.

Every answer but placating ends a plot.

A grab comes to a head 26 weeks after the gossip. With a board, it succeeds when the executive has courted more than half the seats and has more influence than you. Then they take the chair if they courted every seat and their influence beats yours by 25 or more: you are ousted and the run ends. Otherwise they become co-CEO, which costs one step of creative freedom now and at every review. A failed grab (or any grab with no board) sends them to a rival, and they try to take the least loyal artist, who goes with them if that loyalty is below 50.

**Company value and IPO.** The Wallet shows your share of 10,000 shares. Company value combines a year of revenue times a phase, growth, and hit-rate multiple, plus catalog value and cash, minus debt. In phase 5, an IPO requires three consecutive profitable years, a CFO with at least 60 skill, and board confidence of at least 60. The IPO sells 25% of the company. Its price reflects valuation and scandal history. In phase 6, analysts publish half-year revenue targets. Their results move market mood, share price, and board confidence. Board reviews also move to 26-week intervals.

**Acquisitions.** In phase 6, buy a rival's catalog or a small label from the Company panel. The catalog pays weekly royalties, and a small label sends an artist into a free roster slot. Pay the price in cash or finance it with 35% down. A majority board vote is required when a deal exceeds your remaining budget.

**Final review.** A CEO run ends after 20 years, dismissal, or bankruptcy. Company value, your equity, hits, awards, artists developed, and whether you held the chair contribute to the grade. Any CEO ending allows a CEO New Game+ run with earned legacy points.

**Classic conversion.** Earlier labels can stay in Classic or convert on load. Conversion keeps the year, cash, roster, records, and history, maps rank to company phase, moves rooms and facilities into buildings, and turns existing staff into department heads. Converted phases with board seats start at 60 confidence. Export the original from **Export classic backup**.

**One ladder.** In CEO runs the company phase does the job the label rank does in classic saves: roster size (2, 3, 5, 7, 8 and 10 artists), themes per album (one, then two from phase 3), and the room tier (1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 4). Reaching a phase pays its backers ($6,000 at phase 2 up to $100,000 at phase 6) and finds two new themes (three at phase 6). There are no yearly goals; the yearly review still sums up each year.

**Roster tiers.** Artists are up-and-comers, stars or superstars. Each phase sets the slots (up-and-comer / star / superstar): 2/0/0, 2/1/0, 3/1/1, 4/2/1, 4/2/2, 5/3/2. Every signing starts as an up-and-comer and needs a free up-and-comer slot. An artist moves up at the weekly settlement once they have the fame and hits for the next tier and a slot is free there: star needs 40 fame and one hit (quality 75+) with the label, superstar 75 fame and three hits. Two releases in a row below quality 55 drop a star or superstar one tier.

A tier caps what the company spends on the artist: up-and-comers record on budgets up to $4,000, run ads up to street posters and radio, tour regionally and have a team of two; stars go up to $8,000, billboard ads, national tours and a team of four; superstars up to $12,000, every ad, world tours and a team of seven.

**Superstars, the face of the company.** Each superstar lifts company value (and the share price) by up to 6% at full fame; while they feud with you (relationship below 0) they drag it down by half that. They earn endorsements every week (fame × $10 a week at phase 3, rising to $40 at phase 6; half while feuding). About once every 33 weeks a superstar makes news: good news lifts value 8% for 8 weeks and pays $4,000 × phase, bad news cuts it 10%, costs $3,000 × phase and cools the relationship. Good news is likelier when the relationship is warm.

The relationship runs from −100 to 100. A new superstar starts at 25, more if loyal; each week it drifts toward (morale − 60) ÷ 2. Event choices swing it half again as hard as for other artists: sneaker deals, public call-outs, a vanity imprint, rumours.

**A second act.** Being fired or ousted ends the company, but not always the run. A superstar whose relationship with you is 40 or more and whose loyalty is 50 or more will follow you: you start a new company at phase 1 with them (on a fresh two-album deal), $15,000 from old contacts and a fresh start at week one. With nobody willing, the run's final review is the end.
